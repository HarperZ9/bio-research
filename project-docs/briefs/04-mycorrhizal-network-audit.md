# Brief 04: Evidence audit of common mycorrhizal network (CMN) claims

Date: 2026-10-09. Method: research synthesis (anchor, read, classify, shared framework, labeled inference, result first).
Confidence labels: high / moderate / low / unknown. Evidence-reading depth is stated per source in section 8.

## Result first

The popular "wood wide web" story bundles five claims of very different strength.

| Claim | Verdict | Confidence |
|---|---|---|
| A. Mycorrhizal fungi are shared across neighboring trees and could link them | Supported as potential; continuity in the field is mapped in very few forests | moderate |
| B. Carbon (and other resources) can move between plants via fungi | Supported in lab (autoradiography); field route not isolated from soil pathway | moderate |
| C. Transfer is large enough to matter for the recipient | Weak | moderate |
| D. CMN access improves seedling survival or growth | Mixed (positive, neutral, negative; context dependent) | moderate |
| E. Mother trees preferentially feed kin; trees send warnings | Weak for trees, untested in the field | moderate to high |

Open question that decides most of the rest: in an intact forest, with roots, soil water and hyphae all present,
does net carbon move from an adult tree through a continuous shared fungal genet into a seedling's shoot tissue,
in an amount that changes seedling fitness, beyond what soil and root-exudate pathways deliver? (section 5)

## 1. Question and the decision it informs

Question: which CMN claims rest on field evidence that excludes alternative pathways, and which rest on
inference, greenhouse work, correlation, or repeated citation?

Decisions informed:
- Forest management. Retention of "mother trees" in harvest and regeneration prescriptions is justified by
  retention of shade, seed source, soil, and fungal inoculum anyway. Whether CMN transfer adds a separate
  reason is the open part. Karst et al. conclude CMN knowledge is "too sparse and unsettled to inform forest
  management" (read in full text). Simard et al. argue for managing forests as interconnected systems. [both sides, high confidence that these are their stated positions]
- Public communication. Which sentences can be said without a qualifier (fungi are mutualists of most plants)
  and which need one (trees feed their young).
- Research priorities. Where one experiment would move the field most (section 5), and where citation
  hygiene (section 6) is cheaper than new fieldwork.

## 2. The claims, with strongest support and strongest critique

### Claim A. CMNs are widespread in forests

Strongest support:
- Beiler et al. 2010 mapped two Rhizopogon species and interior Douglas-fir by microsatellite genotyping in a
  30 x 30 m old-growth plot. Each Rhizopogon species formed 13 to 14 genets, each colonizing up to 19 trees,
  linking multiple age cohorts, with a scale-free, hub-dominated topology (abstract read). [high that this is the result reported]
- Mycoheterotrophic plants cannot photosynthesize and must receive carbon through fungi that also associate
  with trees. Merckx et al. 2024 argue this is natural evidence that CMNs are prevalent and move net carbon
  (abstract read, full text not read). Karst et al. also concede mycoheterotrophs are strong evidence of
  existence and transfer, but note popular claims center on trees.
- Many ectomycorrhizal (EcM) fungi are host generalists with genets spanning metres (Karst et al., stated as
  plausible basis for claim A).

Strongest critique (Karst et al. 2023, full text read):
- Genotype mapping of fungi plus plants is the strongest evidence of structure, yet only five studies exist,
  in two forest types, two tree species, three EcM fungi. Only two studies show continuity among trees.
- Same fungal species on two trees does not mean same genet. Hyphae and root tips turn over and are grazed, so
  a mapped link may be a remnant, not a functional connection. Persistence is unknown.
- Inference from shared species lists is routinely cited as presence of CMNs.

Fair reading: potential is high, demonstrated continuity is thin and sampled from few systems. Beiler 2010 maps
co-occurrence of a genet on trees; it does not measure flow. [moderate to high]

### Claim B. Resources move between trees through CMNs

Strongest support:
- Simard et al. 1997 (Nature): reciprocal isotope labeling of paper birch and Douglas-fir seedlings in the
  field gave bidirectional carbon transfer with net gain to Douglas-fir, averaging 6% of carbon isotope
  uptake by photosynthesis; shading changed net transfer; non-EcM western redcedar took up little, read as
  evidence for a hyphal route (abstract read via RePEc; full text and replicate counts UNVERIFIED).
- Warren et al. 2008: stump application of D2O and dye reached ponderosa pine seedlings in 61 micrometre mesh
  chambers (roots excluded); 8 of 10 mesh-chamber seedlings enriched; trenched seedlings 1.8 times less enriched.
  Karst calls this the strongest field evidence for a CMN or root graft route, with unknown effect on performance.
- Song et al. 2015: Douglas-fir donors defoliated, ponderosa pine receivers in pots; carbon transfer through the
  mycorrhizal network but not soil or root pathways (35 micrometre vs 0.5 micrometre vs no mesh).
- Klein, Siegwolf and Koerner 2016: canopy-scale 13CO2 labeling of 40 m spruce; tree-to-tree transfer
  "assisted by common ectomycorrhiza networks" accounted for 40% of fine-root carbon (about 280 kg/ha/yr).

Strongest critique:
- Karst: of 26 field studies (mostly Western North America, 77% EcM, 35% Douglas-fir, most lasting 2 years or
  less), none excludes every alternative. Soil-solution carbon flow, root contact, hyphal extension into
  hydraulically lifted water, and mesh pore size effects on soil volume remain open. They state the Simard 1997,
  Lerat, Teste and Klein results are consistent with the soil pathway.
- Henriksson et al. 2023 (abstract read): isotope studies can make "important inferences based on miniscule
  shifts in isotopic abundance"; evidence of significant net C transfer via CMNs that benefits recipients is
  "still lacking"; a fungal C pipeline between trees is hard to reconcile with fungal adaptive advantage.
- Pickles et al. 2017: 0.5 or 35 micrometre mesh did not restrict 13C transfer between Douglas-fir seedlings,
  and fungi held 60 to 70% of enriched phospholipids. This supports an exudate-hyphal uptake pathway, which
  is not tree-to-tree sharing.

Simard, Ryan and Perry 2025 reply (page read through offset 100000 of 129075 characters): multiple pathways
operate at once; about 18% of isotope reached AM redcedar relative to EcM-EcM transfer, argued as hard to
explain by root density alone; shoot transfer was reported in several studies (they cite Teste 2009, Philip
2010, Song 2015, Klein 2016, Cahanovitc 2022). The reply is by the authors of the criticized work. [high that these arguments were made; moderate on my paraphrase]

### Claim C. Amounts matter

- Teste et al. 2009 (160 plots, interior Douglas-fir, 13C and 15N pulse labeling of older donors): seed-sown
  seedlings without mesh got 0.0063% of donor photoassimilate carbon and 0.0018% of nitrogen; larger donors
  transferred less. Authors still conclude MNs and roots may matter for natural regeneration.
- Simard 1997: net 6% of seedling photosynthetic uptake (small seedlings, shaded conditions). Klein 2016: 40% of
  fine-root carbon, in a pooled mixing-model estimate not a seedling subsidy.
- Critique: Henriksson argues no demonstrated net benefit to recipients. Näsholm et al. 2013 find that EcM
  fungi may keep most absorbed N and pay little to trees under N limitation, so fungi are not passive pipes.
- Inference (mine, low confidence): fractions differ by orders of magnitude (0.006% to 40%) because
  denominators differ (donor assimilate, recipient uptake, fine-root pool). They are not comparable without
  restating them as recipient carbon budget fractions.

### Claim D. CMN access raises seedling survival and growth

Support: Booth and Hoeksema 2010 (monodominant Pinus radiata forest, experimental test) found large positive
network effects on survival with simultaneous negative root effects, and better water access. Teste 2009: survival
best without mesh for seed-sown seedlings, not planted ones. Birch et al. 2021: adult Douglas-fir growth rose
with number of R. vinicolor connections (correlational, on the Beiler plot). Simard et al. also cite Barker 2013
and Bingham and Simard 2011 (second-hand via reply; not opened).

Critique: Karst, across 13 studies with a no-mesh treatment (28 experiments), only 5 (18%) showed significant
positive CMN effects not offset by negative root effects; results are positive, neutral, and negative, with
neutral most common. Fine-mesh bags reduce soil volume, so lower survival need not mean lost CMN access. No field
study characterized pathogen and mycorrhizal community changes from the mesh treatments. Pec et al. 2020
(lodgepole pine, mesh cylinders): networks helped seedlings in undisturbed stands and shifted to a cost where
beetle mortality was high; Karst offers a pathogen explanation. Schoonmaker et al. 2007: no detectable difference in
hydraulically redistributed water between soil-plus-CMN and soil-only pathways.

### Claim E. Mother trees favor kin; trees send warning signals

Support:
- Pickles et al. 2017: full-sibling Douglas-fir pairs showed three to fourfold greater 13C transfer to
  recipient roots (about 4 micrograms excess 13C) in 2 of 4 families. Greenhouse, seedlings, pathway not
  restricted by mesh.
- Song et al. 2010 (tomato, AM fungus Glomus mosseae): disease resistance and defense enzymes rose in healthy
  receivers when donors were infected; Song et al. 2015 reported defense-enzyme increases in ponderosa pine
  receivers via the network. Both are pot studies. Simard et al. also cite a thesis (Gorzelak 2017) on kin-biased
  defense signals (not opened).
- Beiler 2010 hub structure (large trees most connected) is cited as basis for "mother trees". It is a
  topology result.

Critique: Karst found no peer-reviewed field evidence for either preferential kin transfer or warning signals
via CMNs; the signaling effect in the one tree pot study disappeared when roots interacted; the single peer-reviewed
kin study found transfer through soil solution; the claim leans on graduate theses; the one study of
125 to 275 year old trees to conspecific seedlings does not control the soil pathway. Robinson et al. 2024 call
the mother-tree concept inconclusive and driven by personification. Merckx 2024 and Karst agree herbaceous or
mycoheterotroph systems differ.

Note on independence: Henriksson 2023 and Robinson 2024 share an author (Polle); Karst co-authored with
Simard on Birch 2021 and Pec 2020. The camps are not fully independent of each other. Karst reports some
unsupported citations came from her own earlier papers.

## 4. Ecology that stands regardless

Solid ground (most checked against abstracts; textbook items noted):
- Most land plants are mycorrhizal: about 72% arbuscular (AM), 2% ectomycorrhizal (EcM), 1.5% ericoid, 10%
  orchid, 8% non-mycorrhizal (Brundrett and Tedersoo 2018). [high]
- AM symbiosis is an exchange: plants reward better fungal partners with more carbohydrate, and fungi increase
  nutrient transfer to roots that pay more (Kiers et al. 2011). Control is bidirectional. [high for AM systems; applicability to EcM trees is inference, moderate]
- EcM fungi are not passive conduits. In N-limited boreal pine they immobilized soil N and passed little to trees
  (Nasholm et al. 2013). Fungi can aggravate or alleviate limitation by conditions. [moderate to high]
- PNW Douglas-fir. Rhizopogon vesiculosus and R. vinicolor are dominant EcM fungi in interior Douglas-fir
  forests; R. vesiculosus mycelia occur deeper, are more pervasive, and colonize more roots; both concentrate in
  moisture-retaining microsites (Beiler 2012; Beiler 2010). Rhizopogon is a genus of truffle-like (hypogeous)
  fungi [from memory, UNVERIFIED in this session]. These results are from interior (dry, BC) Douglas-fir, not
  coastal PNW forests. [high on the interior result; unknown for coastal]
- Mycoheterotrophs prove net carbon moves from fungus to plant in some systems (Merckx 2024, Karst). [high on principle]
- Roots compete with seedlings; net belowground interactions among trees are typically negative per Karst's
  trenching review (their cited review not opened here). [moderate]

Implication: a CMN can exist, fungi can move resources among hosts, and EcM matters to Douglas-fir nutrition.
None of this entails directed tree-to-tree altruism.

## 5. Verifier design for the key contested claim

Claim to settle: net carbon moves from an adult tree through a shared fungal genet to a seedling and changes
seedling fitness (claims B plus C plus D together).

Design (inference from Karst Box 3 and Fig. 2, Henriksson, and the studies above; design choices mine):
1. Site: interior Douglas-fir stand with known Rhizopogon, adult donors with measured root maps.
2. Link verification: genotype Rhizopogon on donor root tips and on each seedling's root tips; classify each
   donor-seedling pair as shared genet or not (Teste 2008 found 1 of 4 pairs shared, per Karst).
3. Treatments per donor-seedling pair: (a) intact soil, roots and hyphae; (b) hyphae only, single mesh of
   20 to 44 micrometre inside trenched large soil volume (avoids pore-size volume confound); (c) multilayer
   mesh with air gap to block bulk soil water flow (Karst Fig. 2d); (d) 0.5 micrometre mesh or severed
   hyphae (soil solution control); (e) AM-only recipient species control; (f) shared genet versus non-shared
   genet pairs within (b).
4. Labeling: 13CO2 pulse to donors, with unlabeled reference trees for natural abundance baselines and
   mass balance; track 13C in recipient root tips, fungal biomass (PLFA or NLFA), and shoot tissue separately.
5. Fitness: survival and biomass across at least 3 growing seasons, plus pathogen and EcM community sequencing
   in every treatment.
6. Pre-register effect thresholds and sample size (power calculation from Teste 2009 variance; not computed here).

Results that would support the claim (all required): shoot 13C excess in (b) shared-genet pairs above (c) and
(d) by the pre-registered margin; excess not explained by fungal tissue enrichment; transferred carbon above a
stated fraction of seedling carbon budget (threshold to be set before the data, my suggestion 1% of annual
seedling carbon gain); and a survival or growth difference tied to link status.

Results that would refute (for that system): transfer in (b) equal to (c) or (d); enrichment limited to
fungal biomass and root tips; shared-genet pairs not different from non-shared; no fitness link. An intermediate
result (transfer yes, fitness no) supports claim B and refutes C and D.

Why the verifier can be fooled (check the checks): a positive signal can come from fungal biomass retention,
root exudates, or mesh effects on pathogens; a null can come from short duration (Karst: most experiments 2
years or less) or sparse link frequency. Include blind sample handling and a pre-specified analysis.

## 7. Does not prove, and risks

This audit does not prove:
- That CMNs do not transfer meaningful resources. Absence of definitive field evidence is not evidence of absence.
  Karst says so directly.
- That Simard's results are wrong. The 1997 transfer was measured; the dispute is route and magnitude.
- Anything about coastal PNW forests beyond what interior BC Douglas-fir studies imply.
- Anything about boreal, tropical, or AM-dominated systems, where most evidence comes from other studies.

Risks:
- Reading depth: Karst was read in full; Frontiers reply 100,000 of 129,075 characters; Henriksson, Simard 1997,
  Klein, Booth, Teste, Pec, Birch, Song, Pickles, Merckx, Robinson abstracts only. Sample sizes and statistics
  marked UNVERIFIED were not in abstracts.
- Partisan sources: both main camps include authors of work being judged. Counts (26, 18, 13, 28, 5) are Karst's.
  The Frontiers reply says 18 studies and an inflated 272; I did not verify that arithmetic. The Karst text I read states 18
  influential studies and 593 plus 1,083 citing papers. Treat the reply's counting objection as unresolved.
- Selection: I did not systematically search all CMN literature. Studies by neither camp (for example, the
  Karst-cited trenching review and AM-system work) are under-sampled. The Karst Author Correction
  (10.1038/s41559-023-02035-7) exists; I did not read it.
- Management risk: acting on either extreme. Removing legacy trees because "mother trees" are myth overlooks
  shade, seed, inoculum and soil values; retaining them as proven carbon donors overstates the case.
- Communication risk: the personification critique (Robinson 2024) and the personification-friendly framing
  both shape public belief; sentence-level wording matters.
